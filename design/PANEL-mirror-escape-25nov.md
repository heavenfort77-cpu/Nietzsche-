### Story writer (Opus)

Seat: Story writer (story-writer)
What the Mirror World says today (one line): "Face your insecurities and beat them each night." That is a self-help arc with a fresh win every night, and it contradicts both the theme and Dazai's Yozo, who never faces anything.
What it would say as an escape (one line): "You cannot beat what is in you. You can only buy room from it with the same clowning that hollows you, and keep moving." That is Yozo's clowning as evasion, played out.
Carries the theme, 1-5: today 2, as escape 4
E1: YES. Running from figures you cannot beat, paid for in Laughs, makes the game the pursuer and makes every gag a small performance bought with the self. The play says the meaning, not the captions.
E2: YES. Running works for two nights, then the structure refuses it: every exit is the ring. That is the strongest structural line in the game, and "I quit" becomes a choice instead of a loot screen. The third return must look different from the first two (lights, crowd, the child), or players will read the loop as a level bug.
Harshest objection to the proposal: if the way out is open from the start and gags are optional, a skilled player slips past without clowning once. Then the core statement ("you only get room by performing") never happens in their hands, and the Mirror is just a corridor runner with a theme pasted on.
One change to the proposal (within E1/E2 only; or "no change" with a reason): every space's way out sits behind at least one figure, so no space can be left without at least one gag. Also change the entry line "These are his insecurities. Face each one." to match escape. Today it promises the fight the proposal removes.

### Dazai (Opus)

Seat: Dazai (author of No Longer Human, judging truth to Yozo)
What the Mirror World says today (one line): "Face your insecurities and you can drive them off": a self-help sermon. Yozo never once faced anything; I would not have written him a victory, even a small one.
What it would say as an escape (one line): The clown's gags never conquer the fear, they only buy a moment's room before it closes in again. That is my clowning exactly, "my last courtship of humanity", a way of being let off for two seconds at a time.
Carries the theme, 1-5: today 2, as escape 4
E1: YES. Yozo runs (to drink, to women, to morphine, to the next lodging), and the world follows him through every door. A fight makes him a hero, and he was never one.
E2: YES. In my book his last flight ends in a room he cannot leave. The ring that every exit returns to is that room, and "I quit" is the first act he does not perform.
Harshest objection to the proposal: "The way out is visible and open from the start" is too kind. For Yozo every escape opened onto a worse room, never onto relief. If each "Get out." feels like getting away, the player learns that running works, and nights 1-2 lie to set up the last night instead of foretelling it.
One change to the proposal (within E2 only): On the last night the Performer does not attack or drain Laughs at all. He only stands in the ring each time the clown runs back in, and the third return is simply the clown arriving at himself. Yozo was not beaten by an enemy; he was put where he had always been.

### Chaplin (Opus)

What it would say as an escape (one line): The little man never beats the cop. He ducks, trips the pursuer and runs for the next door, and they are always behind him. That is my hall-of-mirrors chase in The Circus.
Carries the theme, 1-5: today 3, as escape 5

### Clinical psychologist (Opus)

Seat: Clinical psychologist (avoidance, shame and insecurity)
What the Mirror World says today (one line): Insecurities are enemies you can drive off with jokes each night. Humour as a defence that works for a while is true, but "Face each one" plus a door that opens only once they are beaten makes it a story of mastering them, and shame is not mastered by hitting it.
What it would say as an escape (one line): He gets by on avoidance: running and joking buy room and leave the fear untouched. Nights 1-2 give the short-term relief, and night 3 hits the wall that avoidance always reaches, a self he cannot step out of.
Carries the theme, 1-5: today 3, as escape 4
E1: YES. The gags as safety behaviours, paid in the same Laughs he lives on and holding a figure only about 2 s, are the most accurate thing in the game. Being chased fits "out to get you" better than an arena does.
E2: YES. Shame is about the self, not about one act, so you cannot outrun your own double. Every exit leading back into the ring shows that truthfully, and only if night 3 already felt like being chased.
Harshest objection to the proposal: Getting out pays glass and costs nothing else, so on nights 1-2 the game rewards avoidance with no visible long-term cost. That is half the cycle. As written, the turn on night 3 feels like a new rule rather than the cost of running finally coming due. Also, after "the Performer reaches him", "I quit" plus walking out into black can read as one more escape, or, given the Dazai source, as suicide. The ending has to show him leaving the mask, not leaving life.
One change to the proposal (within E1/E2 only): On night 3, the Performer does not catch him after the third return. The player has to stop running and stand still, the one thing that has always gnawed at him. Then the Performer walks up while his Laughs drain. The ending starts because he chose to stop, which is the moment that heals in real avoidance, and not because he was caught.

## Build facts given to the seats (/tmp/panel44/facts.md, verbatim)

#### The Clown and the Mirror: what the game is and does (build b9ef341, nights 1-3 prototype, 25 Nov 2026)

A 3D browser game (phone and desktop) made for a game jam whose theme is "the game is out to get you". Nights 1-3 are playable as a box-and-shape prototype; art is partly final (the clowns and circus crew are finished 3D models; props and some figures are boxes).

##### One night, in order (what is built)
1. Title: a closed curtain, "Night 1". He sits at a dressing-table mirror; the player's first input finishes painting his clown face ("He paints the smile on."). A short quote card (a line from Osamu Dazai's "No Longer Human") shows before each show. He puffs himself up in a show of bravado; his reflection copies it exactly. Line: "He is the clown now. The act board is next."
2. The changing room: an act board on the wall. He picks one act from paper notes: his own act "The fall" ("Yours. It always pays a little."), plus one act he may use tonight for free ("Light sweep: Yours for tonight, free."); acts he bought later.
3. The walk out: fixed-camera corridor shots ("Hall", "Ring door"). Circus crew, one action each, one goal line per shot: a rigger lays a rope ("Over the rope he goes, and up again."); the props girl gives him a pie ("Pie in hand: one free pie throw in the Mirror."); the strongman hands him a horn ("HONK! The strong man laughs out loud."); a stagehand's child watches him trip on purpose (caption "He trips, and he meant to. The child saw.") and quietly says "...on purpose." Then his own thought shows over the picture: "I do it to feel I belong. But when the act ends, whether it went well or not, I always feel I have lost a part of myself." Crew lines are warm on nights 1-2 and only polite on night 3.
4. The act in the centre ring (camera low behind the crowd; the crowd is backs of heads; the child watches from the entrance and never laughs).
   - The fall (night 1's own act): goal "Stand in the light. Fall when the gold button lights up." He walks into a spotlight pool; standing in it starts a drumroll; the band leader's stick rises; a gold "Fall" button is dim, fills while the drum rolls and lights up "Fall now!" for a quarter second around the crash. Pressing then makes his trip land on the crash. Results: "Perfect. He hits the floor with the crash." / "Good. Close to the crash." / "Not in the light." / "Too early: wait for the drum." / "Too early. He was already down when the crash came." / "Too late. He fell into silence." / "Too late. The crash hit a clown still standing." Three falls; two misses end the act ("Two misses. The act ends."). A Perfect pays 10 Laughs and one more layer of face paint (Mask); a Good pays 5. The spotlight pool moves to a new place after each fall. On night 3 the whiteface calls "Now!" too early; the crash never lies.
   - Other acts (light sweep, talking scale, soldier drill, tit-for-tat) are timing games on an overlay screen, then the routine plays in the ring. In those acts the crowd pays extra for what hurt him (a fall, the pie in his own face, a trip the front rows saw).
   - After every act: the laughter stops, he stands alone in the light with a sad face; then he smiles and bows with his hat back on, dented. Caption: the crowd's reaction, Laughs, Mask.
5. The walk back: same corridor, empty. The more Laughs the act earned, the lower he slumps. The room is gloomier; flakes fall. One goal line per shot ("Walk back toward the camera, into the wings." and so on).
6. The mirror: he presses his hand to the glass; the reflection is a beat late; the glass cracks. A Nietzsche quote card.
7. The Mirror World: entry line "These are his insecurities. Face each one." A route through unworldly spaces (a mirror corridor, a photograph room or a pencil-drawn void) to an arena. Figures there are his insecurities, spawned by what he did in the act: each fall in the act feeds the Whisper; a Perfect brings the Stare; a miss brings Shame; the soldier drill brings the Sergeant; the talking scale the Weigher; the Light is always there. A hint line names how to beat the nearest one ("Hit it from the side.", "Dodge to shake it off.", "Three gags on its lamp.").
   - Laughs are both his health and his ammunition: each gag (slap, pie, a dodge-fall) costs Laughs, shown on the button.
   - Standing still is never safe: the screen edge darkens at once and after a 1 s warning his Laughs drain ("they gnaw") until he acts.
   - The Light can only be put out for the night (three gags on its lamp), never beaten; the dark lamp keeps following him; it returns next night 15% wider.
   - Figures put down are driven off, not killed: each turns its back and walks out of the space. Nothing drops glass (shards). Each space he gets out of pays 4 glass ("He got through. +4 glass."); coming out of the night pays 4 more. A space's door opens only when its group has been driven off. Losing (Laughs at 0) wakes him at the mirror with half his glass. The figures come back every night.
8. The shop at the mirror: he buys acts with shards. "Every purchase adds a paint layer, and the Performer learns it." The paint goes on his face at the sale. Wiping an act off refunds half and leaves a stain.
9. End card: a list of the night's costs, "He got up.", and "Go on to night N".

Meters: Laughs (earned on stage, spent in the Mirror World, do not carry between nights). Mask: paint layers, earned by Perfects and by every purchase; more Mask makes the Mirror World warp more and makes the Performer stronger.

##### The last night (night 3, built as box and shape)
He has lost every act he bought: only slap, pie and the dodge-fall are left. He fights the night's insecurities, then the Performer, his pale double, who attacks with every act he bought (each attack has a tell); more Mask = more Performer health. When the Performer is beaten it shatters (flash, glass sound); black and silence; a last Nietzsche card ("I refuse to be a saint; I would rather be a clown.").
