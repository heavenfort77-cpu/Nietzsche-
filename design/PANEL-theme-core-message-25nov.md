### Story writer (Opus)

1. What the play alone says about life: you paint a smile on and offer the only thing you have, falling down for people. The world pays most for what hurts you, so even failure is eaten as the act. What you earn in public is all you have to defend yourself in private, and defence costs the same coin, so it is never enough. At night's end you have nothing, are told that is shameful, and get up anyway.

2. Core message: every act you perform to belong costs a piece of yourself, and the world only pays for your falls. Score: 3. The stage, the walk back and the end card say this; the warm strongman and the Mirror World's kill-and-loot loop do not.

3. Hands score: 4. The spotlight walking off him while he stands still, and a self-defence drop costing 9 Laughs, say "out to get you" through the fingers.

4. Harshest objection: the Mirror World tells another story. "Face each one", beat it, take its shard, spend it in a shop: a self-help arc where insecurities can be defeated. 5. One change: no Mirror World insecurity is ever killed, only driven off for the night like the Light, returning next night; shards pay for lasting the night, not for slaying. Then every fight says what "He got up." says.

### Clinical psychologist (Opus)

1. What the play alone says about life: You put the face on before anyone asks, and the only way you know to be with people is to fall for them. Approval arrives as currency, not as connection, and it runs out the moment you are alone. Even your real failure gets eaten as part of the act, so there is no moment where the true self is seen, except by one child who names it. Then you are handed a bill called shame, and you get up anyway.

2. Core message: performing for approval buys just enough to survive yourself, and empties you a little each time. Score: 3. The stage, walk back and end card say this; the Mirror's action controls, the warm strongman and failure paying +15 pull toward other games.

3. Hands score: 3. The moving spotlight and the drain when standing still feel like life pressing in, but much of the hostility came from unclear keys and blocked corridors, which a player blames on the game, not on life.

4. Harshest objection: in my clients, masking only traps people because the applause genuinely feels good first. The relief is the hook; the emptiness is the cost. This player never landed a fall, never felt the laugh land, so the hollowness afterwards was only told ("The shame of living"), never felt as a contrast. Shame without a preceding high reads as punishment, not as the slow cost of a mask that works.

5. One change: make the first fall of night 1 near-certain to land Perfect, with a real, warm, felt payoff (roar, light, his posture lifting), so the slump on the walk back is the loss of something the player actually had.

## Build facts given to the seats (/tmp/panel/facts.md, verbatim)

#### The Clown and the Mirror: what the game is and does (build d8e5541, nights 1-3 prototype, 25 Nov 2026)

A 3D browser game (phone and desktop) made for a game jam whose theme is "the game is out to get you". Nights 1-3 are playable as a box-and-shape prototype; art is partly final (the clowns and circus crew are finished 3D models; props and some figures are boxes).

##### One night, in order (what is built)
1. Title: a closed curtain, "Night 1". He sits at a dressing-table mirror; the player's first input finishes painting his clown face ("He paints the smile on."). A short quote card (a line from Osamu Dazai's "No Longer Human") shows before each show. He puffs himself up in a show of bravado; his reflection copies it exactly. Line: "He is the clown now. The act board is next."
2. The changing room: an act board on the wall. He picks one act from paper notes: his own act "The fall" ("Yours. It always pays a little."), plus one act he may use tonight for free ("Light sweep: Yours for tonight, free."); acts he bought later.
3. The walk out: fixed-camera corridor shots ("Hall", "Ring door"). Circus crew, one action each, one goal line per shot: a rigger lays a rope ("Over the rope he goes, and up again."); the props girl gives him a pie ("Pie in hand: one free pie throw in the Mirror."); the strongman hands him a horn ("HONK! The strong man laughs out loud."); a stagehand's child watches him trip on purpose (caption "He trips, and he meant to. The child saw.") and quietly says "...on purpose." Then his own thought shows over the picture: "I do it to feel I belong. But when the act ends, whether it went well or not, I always feel I have lost a part of myself." Crew lines are warm on nights 1-2 and only polite on night 3.
4. The act in the centre ring (camera low behind the crowd; the crowd is backs of heads; the child watches from the entrance and never laughs).
   - The fall (night 1's own act): goal "Stand in the light. Fall when the gold button lights up." He walks into a spotlight pool; standing in it starts a drumroll; the band leader's stick rises; a gold "Fall" button is dim, fills while the drum rolls and lights up "Fall now!" for a quarter second around the crash. Pressing then makes his trip land on the crash. Results: "Perfect. He hits the floor with the crash." / "Good. Close to the crash." / "Not in the light." / "Too early: wait for the drum." / "Too early. He was already down when the crash came." / "Too late. He fell into silence." / "Too late. The crash hit a clown still standing." Three falls; two misses end the act ("Two misses. The act ends."). A Perfect pays 10 Laughs and one more layer of face paint (Mask); a Good pays 5. The spotlight pool moves to a new place after each fall. On night 3 the whiteface calls "Now!" too early; the crash never lies.
   - Other acts (light sweep, talking scale, soldier drill, tit-for-tat) are timing games on an overlay screen, then the routine plays in the ring. In those acts the crowd pays extra for what hurt him (a fall, the pie in his own face, a trip the front rows saw).
   - After every act: the laughter stops, he stands alone in the light with a sad face; then he smiles and bows with his hat back on, dented. Caption: the crowd's reaction, Laughs, Mask.
5. The walk back: same corridor, empty. The more Laughs the act earned, the lower he slumps. The room is gloomier; flakes fall. One goal line per shot ("Walk back toward the camera, into the wings." and so on).
6. The mirror: he presses his hand to the glass; the reflection is a beat late; the glass cracks. A Nietzsche quote card.
7. The Mirror World: entry line "These are his insecurities. Face each one." A route through unworldly spaces (a mirror corridor, a photograph room or a pencil-drawn void) to an arena. Figures there are his insecurities, spawned by what he did in the act: each fall in the act feeds the Whisper; a Perfect brings the Stare; a miss brings Shame; the soldier drill brings the Sergeant; the talking scale the Weigher; the Light is always there. A hint line names how to beat the nearest one ("Hit it from the side.", "Dodge to shake it off.", "Three gags on its lamp.").
   - Laughs are both his health and his ammunition: each gag (slap, pie, a dodge-fall) costs Laughs, shown on the button.
   - Standing still is never safe: the screen edge darkens at once and after a 1 s warning his Laughs drain ("they gnaw") until he acts.
   - The Light can only be put out for the night (three gags on its lamp), never beaten; the dark lamp keeps following him; it returns next night 15% wider.
   - Beaten figures drop shards. Losing wakes him at the mirror with half his shards.
8. The shop at the mirror: he buys acts with shards. "Every purchase adds a paint layer, and the Performer learns it." The paint goes on his face at the sale. Wiping an act off refunds half and leaves a stain.
9. End card: "The shame of living:" and a list of the night's costs; "Go on to night N".

Meters: Laughs (earned on stage, spent in the Mirror World, do not carry between nights). Mask: paint layers, earned by Perfects and by every purchase; more Mask makes the Mirror World warp more and makes the Performer stronger.

##### The last night (night 3, built as box and shape)
He has lost every act he bought: only slap, pie and the dodge-fall are left. He fights the night's insecurities, then the Performer, his pale double, who attacks with every act he bought (each attack has a tell); more Mask = more Performer health. When the Performer is beaten it shatters (flash, glass sound); black and silence; a last Nietzsche card ("I refuse to be a saint; I would rather be a clown."). A warm light of his own opens on him in the middle of the ring, his paint still on; the monster Light's dark lamp is still in the ring, turning after him with its click. The audience appears one by one, clapping faster and faster, held, then cut to silence. Two worded buttons: (1) "Bow, say "I quit" and walk out": his paint comes off while he bows, "I quit." on screen, he walks to the ring's edge where the child stands, black; (2) "Perform one last act": three falls in the light, no laugh, no words, no numbers, black. After either, 2.5 s of black and silence, then only "Start again from night 1".
