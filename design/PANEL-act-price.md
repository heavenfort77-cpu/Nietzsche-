# Panel: the act after the cue, a real clown gag that costs him his dignity (22 Nov, session 15)

Owner (22 Nov 13:45), replacing merged B's "replay the beats as gags": "i mean an actual gag play out, we have research on actual clowns gag? the cue is to determine how much he gain, the act is base on actual clown gag to show how much he lose of himself. my take is most slapstick are funny but it is also degrading. i want to show he succeeded with a price, his dignity and that is also part of why he walk back dejected." Also agreed: the small-win Mask rule (written into the live GDD Meters section, rev 135).

Sources the judges used: the GDD acts table (each act is a documented routine) and the Muybridge index (plates by gag). The routines report itself is in the session pack, not in the Project or the repo; the two night-1 acts are well documented:
- **The deliberate fall:** Dazai, *No Longer Human*: at the gymnasium Yozo misses the horizontal bar on purpose and lands on his backside in the sand; the class laughs; Takeichi alone whispers "On purpose. On purpose." The circus pratfall is the same gag.
- **Spotlight sweep:** Emmett Kelly (Weary Willie, Ringling Bros.): the tramp clown finds the spotlight's circle on the ring floor and tries to sweep it up with a broom; the circle shrinks with each sweep until he sweeps the last of it under a rug, and the ring goes dark.
- Muybridge plates for the moves: sitting down hard on the ground (series 56), getting up after a fall (57), back somersault (19), shovelling for the sweep (38), kicking a hat (46), bow with arms up (65).

Method: the same 12 judges, proposal, counter-proposal and vote until 7 of 12 agree. Judgement of design, not a playtest.

## Round 1: proposals

| # | Proposal | From |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Owner's take as stated: cue first (decides the Laughs), then the act's real routine plays out in the ring at the zoom-out; the routine shows what he gives up. | Owner, jam judge, indie critic |
| P2 | Each cue chain drives one segment of the routine (night 1: 3 chains = 3 segments). A Perfect makes the segment land fully: the bigger laugh, and the more complete humiliation. A miss is the real accident, which (fix 2) gets the biggest laugh of all. | Core gamer, Chaplin |
| P3 | The price carries over: the walk-back slump is set by how much the crowd laughed at him in the act. The more he earned, the lower he walks back. No new meter (the GDD keeps two). | Clinical psychologist, Camus |
| P4 | The beat after: when the laugh dies the camera holds about 1.5 s on him alone in the spot, still on the floor (or with the broom in the dark); his face shows the sad cell for that hold only, then the smile comes back for the bow. | Dazai, Chaplin |
| P5 | The hat is his dignity: the bowler is knocked off, crushed or swept up in every routine; he puts it back on, dented, for the bow and wears it dented on the walk back. | Professional auguste clown |
| P6 | Play the routine live under the cue instead of after it (the timing drives the slapstick directly). | Indie critic, streamer |

## Round 2: counter-proposals

- Core gamer against P2 and P3 together: if a Perfect makes him suffer more and slump lower, good timing feels punished. Psychologist's answer: the gain is mechanical and real (Laughs are his health and ammo in the Mirror); the price is shown, never charged as a stat. That is the owner's "succeeded with a price". Core gamer accepted, as long as no number goes down for timing well.
- Chaplin on P2: the gag has to be funny first, or it reads as cruelty. The degradation lands only in P4's beat after, once the laughter has stopped. Streamer agreed: the clip is the silence after the laugh.
- Dazai against making the crowd cruel: in the book nobody is cruel; they simply laugh, and only one person sees. The child from the corridor stands at the ring entrance during the act and is the one figure who does not laugh. Adopted into P4.
- Auguste clown against undressing gags (dropped trousers): the hat says it without making the player look away. P5 kept, trousers out.
- Mobile producer on length: the routine is 6-9 s on night 1 (three segments of about 2 s plus the 1.5 s hold), skippable after its first full play.
- Against P6 (cue and gag at once): the cue needs the zoom in on his face; the routine needs the zoom out on his body and the crowd. Doing both at once loses one of them. Owner's order kept: cue, then routine. Indie critic withdrew P6; streamer kept it as a later idea for the final night.
- Jam judge on the walk back: P3 makes the walk back the result screen, which the GDD wants (bodies, not text). The crowd caption stays one line.
- Nietzsche: the hat that comes back dented each night is the recurrence; P5 should persist across nights (the dent stays). Night 1 only has one night, so it is noted for night 2.

## Round 3: vote on the merged proposal

**Merged (P1 + P2 + P3 + P4 + P5):**
1. Walk-on at the zoom-out: he steps into the spotlight, the house quiets (kept from merged B), about 2 s.
2. The cue, unchanged (zoom in). It decides the Laughs, as now.
3. Back to the zoom-out: the act's real routine plays out, one segment per cue chain, in the order played:
   - **The deliberate fall** (night 1): (1) the confident strut to the bar, hat tipped to the house; (2) the swing and the miss, flat on his backside in the sawdust, hat rolls away; (3) he gets up, fetches the hat, bows, and the bow tips him over again.
   - **Spotlight sweep** (night 1, lent): (1) he finds the light on the floor and starts to sweep; (2) it shrinks with every stroke, he chases it on his knees; (3) he sweeps the last of it under the rug, it is dark, and his hat is in the dustpan.
   - Per segment: Perfect = the segment lands fully and the rows rock hardest; Good = it lands smaller; miss = the real accident (fall backwards, item 8), the biggest laugh (item 1, fix 2).
4. The beat after: laughter stops; about 1.5 s on him alone, sad face for that hold only; the child at the ring entrance is the one figure who does not laugh; then the smile and the bow with the hat back on, dented.
5. One-line caption (the fix-2 headline and the Laughs), then the walk back, whose slump is set by the Laughs the act earned (the more, the lower) and with the dented hat.
6. No number goes down for timing well. Tap skips after the first full play.
